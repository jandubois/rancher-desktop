## Assessment

The week reads worse than it is. Failures rose to 437 from 413, but CI also ran 316 more jobs outside the quarantine, and the resulting 4.0% failure rate sits above RD1's 3.2% median while staying well inside the 1.1-6.1% range measured across the 13 weeks from April.

Only one thing here is a real break. Eight of the 17 main-branch failures are the same nightly rddepman death on a deleted extension tag, `julianb90/tachometer:0.1.1`, days 4 through 10 of an 11-day run; PR #10882 bumped the pin on 2026-08-31 and the nightly has been green since. The 35 `yarn lint:nofix` failures are dev breakage too: four Dependabot Go branches with the golangci-lint 2.13.2 bump's new staticcheck findings, fixed in 21f699b40 and merged.

Root-cause `go-proxy-module-fetch` instead of re-running it. It hit 3 jobs against a four-week average of 1.2, and it has kept climbing since this window closed, with 6 more in the first four days of W36, most proven flaky by a green re-run of the same commit. The surface moved too, from the `no buffer space available` that dominated in July to HTTP/2 `INTERNAL_ERROR` and DNS failures against proxy.golang.org, which points at a worsening network path to the module proxy.

Nothing else moved.

<details>
<summary>How this report is built</summary>

`ci-sweep weekly --week 2026-W35` over `sweep.db`, one row per **failed job** — a run can fail on one matrix leg out of many, so the job is the unit.

- **Known flake** — a job whose log matched a `matchers.yaml` pattern. Pattern matching only, no LLM.
- **One flake per job** — when several matchers fire, the job counts under the one whose surface appears **earliest** in the log, because the first failure is the root and what follows is fallout: a suite killed at its wall-clock cap logs a failed wait on the way out. The others are recorded but not counted, and only a true tie is left uncounted.
- **Proven flake** — the identical job passed on a re-run of the same commit, so the code cannot be at fault.
- **Unrecognized** — no matcher fired. On `main` it earns analysis; on a PR branch it earns only a count, because PR failures are dev breakage, not CI health.
- **No log** — the log expired or was never uploaded, so the sweep could not classify the failure. A data gap, not a clean bill.
- **What failed** — for a known flake, the matcher's summary; otherwise the failing test and command, or the job error for a failure that never reached its tests. `--fetch` names the rest, at one download each.
- Counts cover only jobs the sweep recorded. A period never swept reads as zero failures.

</details>

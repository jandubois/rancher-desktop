Assessment ¶

W30 was an ordinary week. Its failure rate outside the quarantine was 3.2%, the exact median of the thirteen weeks since 2026-04-27, and the rise from 372 to 424 raw failures follows 657 more jobs run against a previous week whose 1.1% was the lowest in that series. Only 4 jobs proved flaky by passing on a re-run of the same commit.

The week's real content was breakage rather than flakiness. Thursday 23 July carried most of it, when yarn install --immutable rejected the committed yarn.lock on main from 16:37 and failed 35 jobs across Test, Package and both e2e suites, 25 of them on main, with nothing on those commits building; PR #10699 groups vue with @vue/compiler-sfc so the pair can no longer merge separately.

Two breaks were still open at the window's edge. The Windows signing config omits newly bundled executables, failing 5 jobs all on main with the last at Fri 24 19:42, and the two Dependabot jobs fail every day, the typescript patch since 2026-07-16 and the gomod sibling-module resolution since 2026-05-22. The signing break is one yaml entry away from fixed and is the cheapest thing here to close. Of the week's transient surfaces postinstall-fetch-aborted dominates at 9 jobs, and it is the one worth root-causing rather than re-running.

How this report is built

ci-sweep weekly --week 2026-W30 over sweep.db, one row per failed job — a run can fail on one matrix leg out of many, so the job is the unit.

  • Known flake — a job whose log matched a matchers.yaml pattern. Pattern matching only, no LLM.
  • One flake per job — when several matchers fire, the job counts under the one whose surface appears earliest in the log, because the first failure is the root and what follows is fallout: a suite killed at its wall-clock cap logs a failed wait on the way out. The others are recorded but not counted, and only a true tie is left uncounted.
  • Proven flake — the identical job passed on a re-run of the same commit, so the code cannot be at fault.
  • Unrecognized — no matcher fired. On main it earns analysis; on a PR branch it earns only a count, because PR failures are dev breakage, not CI health.
  • No log — the log expired or was never uploaded, so the sweep could not classify the failure. A data gap, not a clean bill.
  • What failed — for a known flake, the matcher's summary; otherwise the failing test and command, or the job error for a failure that never reached its tests. --fetch names the rest, at one download each.
  • Counts cover only jobs the sweep recorded. A period never swept reads as zero failures.
